Why possums move into roofs

Common brushtail and common ringtail possums look for dry, sheltered spaces. Roof cavities, gaps under tiles, vents and eaves give them shelter, and overhanging branches give them easy access. Once a possum finds a good spot it often returns to it, which is why sealing the entry point matters more than the first sighting.

Signs of possums

  • Scratching, thumping or movement in the ceiling, mostly at dusk and overnight
  • Droppings in the roof space, gutters or on sheds
  • Chewed vents, fascia or tiles, and damaged insulation
  • A strong smell in the roof space or around the eaves

What the law says

Possums are protected under the Wildlife Act 1975 in Victoria. Harming or interfering with them without authority is an offence, and moving a possum to a new location is generally not permitted. Any lethal control requires authorisation from the Department of Energy, Environment and Climate Action (DEECA). We recommend checking the current requirements with DEECA before any action is taken.

Important: Possums with young in the pouch or nest need time to be cared for. Sealing is timed so that dependent young are not trapped inside.

How we handle possum problems

1. Inspection

We check the roof space, eaves, vents and nearby branches to work out where the possum is getting in and whether there are animals or young inside.
