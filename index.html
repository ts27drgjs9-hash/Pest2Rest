Signs you have a pest problem

Many pests are hidden until the problem is well established. These are the signs we look for when we inspect.

Possums

  • Scratching, thumping or movement in the ceiling, mostly at dusk and overnight
  • Droppings in the roof space or on gutters and sheds
  • Chewed or damaged roof vents, fascia or tiles

Rats and mice

  • Scurrying or gnawing in walls and ceilings
  • Droppings in the pantry, under sinks or behind appliances
  • Gnawed packets, cables or pipes

Wasps

  • Steady traffic in and out of one spot, such as a roof gap or wall void
  • A grey papery nest under eaves or in sheds
  • Increased activity around food and bins in warmer months

Bees

  • A humming or buzzing sound from a wall, chimney or roof
  • A cluster of bees on a branch, fence or outside wall
  • Honey or wax leaking from walls or ceilings

How we handle possums under Victorian law

Possums are protected under the Wildlife Act 1975. Harming or interfering with them without authority is an offence, and moving a possum to a new location is generally not permitted. Our approach is built around those rules.

1. Inspection

We identify the animal, work out how it is getting in, and check whether there are young or animals inside before anything else happens.

2. Exclusion

Once the roof space is clear, we seal entry points with durable mesh and metal flashing so possums cannot get back in. Timing takes breeding seasons and dependent young into account.

3. Licensed referral

We don’t trap, harm or move possums ourselves. If a possum is trapped or control is needed, we refer you to a licensed wildlife controller. Any lethal control requires authorisation from DEECA.

Safety and precautions

Every treatment follows the product label and our safety procedures to protect your family, pets and property.

While we treat

  • APVMA-registered products are used only as directed on the label
  • Protective equipment is worn for every job
  • Spraying is kept away from food preparation areas, water sources and play areas
  • Bait stations are locked or placed out of reach of children and pets
  • Occupants are warned and treated areas stay closed until dry

After we leave

  • We tell you how long to keep children and pets away, and when to clean surfaces
  • Unused product and waste are disposed of as the label requires
  • We provide a written record of what was used and where
  • Follow-up visits are offered to check results

Frequently asked questions

Can’t find your answer? Call or message us.

How much does a visit cost?

Possum management starts from $240 and rodent treatment for mice and rats starts at $280. These are starting prices. We confirm the exact quote after inspection, before any work starts.

Do you offer a warranty?

Yes. Our work is covered by a 12-month warranty, with the terms written in your quote before work starts.

Are your products APVMA registered?

We use APVMA-registered products and apply them strictly according to the label. The APVMA registers products, so that is the accurate way to describe our chemicals.

Can you remove a wasp nest?

Yes. We identify the species first, then assess the nest’s location and size and treat it with protective equipment. Nests in difficult spots may need a further visit, which we discuss before work starts.

Do you relocate bee swarms?

We identify whether it’s a swarm or an established hive. Where a beekeeper can collect the colony, we help arrange it. If collection isn’t possible, we discuss treatment with you before anything is done.

Do you offer a same day discount?

Yes. Same day offer: $60 off same day general pest control. Call us to check whether your job qualifies.

Can you remove a possum from my roof?

Possums are protected under the Wildlife Act 1975 in Victoria. We don’t trap, harm or relocate them. We seal entry points so they can’t get back in, and if one is trapped inside we refer you to a licensed wildlife controller.

Is it safe for my kids and pets?

When we follow the label, yes. We tell you how long to keep children and pets away from treated areas, lock bait stations and keep products away from food and water.

Are white-tailed spiders dangerous?

White-tailed spider bites can be painful and may swell or itch. Wash the bite, use a cold pack, and see a doctor if pain spreads or you feel unwell. Call 000 for severe reactions.

How long does treatment take to work?

Many pests start to decline within days. Rodents and possums can take longer, so we schedule follow-up visits to check results.

Do I need to be home?

It helps, so we can explain the work and check access. If you can’t be there, tell us in the enquiry form and we’ll discuss how to arrange it.
